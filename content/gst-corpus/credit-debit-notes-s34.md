# When a credit note is issued

Where a tax invoice has been issued and the taxable value or the tax charged in it is found to exceed the taxable value or tax payable for the supply, or the goods are returned by the recipient, or the goods or services are found to be deficient, the supplier may issue one or more credit notes to the recipient for the supplies made in a financial year. Credit notes against several invoices of a financial year may be issued together.

# Time limit for a credit note

The supplier declares the credit note in the return for the month in which it is issued, and the tax liability is adjusted accordingly, but a credit note cannot be declared later than the thirtieth day of November following the end of the financial year in which the supply was made, or the date of furnishing the annual return for that year, whichever is earlier. No reduction in the output tax liability of the supplier is allowed if the incidence of tax and interest on the supply has been passed on to another person.

# Debit notes

Where the taxable value or tax charged in a tax invoice is found to be less than the taxable value or tax payable, the supplier issues one or more debit notes. A debit note is declared in the return for the month in which it is issued; since 1 January 2021 a debit note need not be linked to a specific invoice, and the recipient's time limit for taking credit on it runs from the debit note's date.

# What a credit or debit note must contain (rule 53)

The name, address and GSTIN of the supplier; the nature of the document; a consecutive serial number of up to sixteen characters unique for a financial year; the date of issue; the recipient's name, address and GSTIN or UIN when registered, or name, address and state otherwise; the serial number and date of the corresponding tax invoice or bill of supply, where the note is linked to one; the value of the taxable supply, the rate of tax and the amount of tax credited or debited; and the signature or digital signature.
