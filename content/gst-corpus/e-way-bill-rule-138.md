# When an e-way bill is needed

Before the movement of goods of consignment value exceeding fifty thousand rupees, in relation to a supply, for reasons other than supply (a job work, a transfer between branches) or an inward supply from an unregistered person, the registered person causing the movement furnishes the details in part A of form GST EWB-01 on the e-way bill portal and, when the goods move by road, part B with the vehicle number. An e-way bill is required regardless of value for goods sent by a principal to a job worker in another state and for handicraft goods moved inter-state by an exempt person. The consignor, the consignee or the transporter may generate it. Several states lower the intra-state threshold or exempt certain goods; the state's own notification applies within the state.

# Validity (rule 138(10))

From 1 January 2021 an e-way bill is valid for one day for every two hundred kilometres or part of it (one day for every twenty kilometres for over-dimensional cargo and multimodal shipments involving ship). The day counts from the time of generation until the end of the next day. The validity may be extended by the transporter within eight hours before or after expiry for exceptional reasons, with the reason recorded.

# Cancellation and changes

An e-way bill may be cancelled within twenty-four hours of generation if the goods are not transported or the details are wrong, but not after it has been verified in transit. Part B (the vehicle) may be updated during the journey. A consignee may reject an e-way bill generated against them within seventy-two hours, after which it is deemed accepted.

# Exemptions (rule 138(14))

No e-way bill is needed for goods in the exempt list (including specified household goods, kerosene, LPG for households, jewellery, currency, used personal effects), goods moved by non-motorised conveyance, goods moved from a port or airport to an inland container depot for customs clearance, movement within a notified area, goods treated as no supply under schedule III, and empty cargo containers, among others.
