# Who must issue e-invoices

A registered person (other than a special economic zone unit, an insurer, a bank or financial institution, a goods transport agency, a passenger transport service, a multiplex cinema, a government department or local authority) whose aggregate turnover in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards exceeded the notified limit must prepare invoices for supplies to registered persons (B2B), exports and supplies to SEZ by uploading them to an invoice registration portal and obtaining an invoice reference number (IRN) and quick response code. The limit was lowered in steps: five hundred crore rupees from 1 October 2020, one hundred crore from 1 January 2021, fifty crore from 1 April 2021, twenty crore from 1 April 2022, ten crore from 1 October 2022, and five crore rupees from 1 August 2023 (notification 10/2023 central tax dated 10 May 2023).

# An invoice without an IRN is not an invoice

For a person to whom e-invoicing applies, a document issued without the IRN is not treated as an invoice (rule 48(5)), and the recipient cannot take credit on it. The e-invoice is sent to the recipient after registration, carrying the QR code; a separate physical signature is not required.

# Time limit for reporting

Invoices must be reported to the invoice registration portal within thirty days of the invoice date for taxpayers with aggregate turnover of one hundred crore rupees or more (from 1 November 2023) and of ten crore rupees or more (from 1 April 2025). Beyond that window the portal rejects the invoice.

# Supplies outside e-invoicing

e-Invoicing does not apply to supplies to unregistered persons (B2C), to nil-rated or exempt supplies, to bills of supply, to delivery challans, or to import bills of entry. A B2C invoice of a person with turnover above five hundred crore rupees must carry a dynamic QR code for payment.
