# 0009 — Reports and question-led dashboards

Status: accepted. Context: prompt 18, PRD FR-070 to FR-073, UX-06, NFR-01.

## Decision

- **A report is a function, not a stored table.** `runReport(db, ctx, id, params, page?)` in `packages/db/src/repositories/reports.ts` turns a business, a period and a few filters into rows and a totals line with grouped SQL over the issued documents, their lines and the payment allocations. Fourteen reports share one catalogue (`REPORTS`: columns, which are summed, which can be grouped, which filters apply, what a row opens). The prompt asked for summary tables or views per business and month, refreshed on write events. We did not build them. The measured cost on 50,000 documents is under 0.6 s for every report on the widest period (two years), so a summary table would add a second copy of every figure, a refresh on each of the issue, cancel, revise, payment and import paths, and a way for the register and the document to disagree. The catalogue and the tests leave room to add one for a single report if it is ever needed (B-042).
- **One rule for what counts.** `packages/domain/src/turnover.ts` now exports the lists (`TURNOVER_PLUS`, `TURNOVER_MINUS`, `PURCHASE_PLUS`, `PURCHASE_MINUS`) that `turnoverSign` and `purchaseSign` read, and the reports build their SQL `IN (...)` lists from the same constants. Issued tax invoices, bills of supply and debit notes add; credit notes subtract; proformas, quotations, challans, drafts and cancelled documents never count. Purchase bills add and purchase returns subtract; expenses have their own report.
- **Figures reconcile by construction and by test.** The sales register, the GST summary (outward), the HSN summary, the party-wise sales, the item-wise sales and the ledger's Sales account give the same net taxable total on the seed, in SQLite and in Postgres. The purchase register equals the GST summary (inward). Party outstanding equals the payment engine's `partyPosition` for every seeded party. The dashboard's "to collect" and "to pay" are the totals of the Party outstanding report they open, so the number on the card is the number at the bottom of the report.
- **Registers page; summaries do not.** A register (sales, purchase, notes, receipts and payments, day book) takes an offset and a limit and returns the totals of the whole filtered set from one aggregate query, so the first 200 rows appear at once and the table loads the next page as it is scrolled. Summaries return every row. Grouping needs every row, so a grouped register loads all of its pages first.
- **Everything is in the address.** `?report=sales_register&preset=this_month&type=credit_note&group=party&cols=date,number,total` is the whole state: the dashboard links, saved views, the command bar actions and a pasted link all open the same thing. Saved views are lists of those searches kept in the `settings` table with the business (`reports.views`), so the owner and an accountant see the same ones.
- **Periods are Indian.** Presets (today, yesterday, this and last month, this and last quarter, this and last financial year) come from `resolvePeriod` in the domain package, quarters follow the financial year (April to June is Q1) and the financial year starts in the business's month. The date is the caller's, from Asia/Kolkata.
- **Exports.** CSV uses `toCsv` (byte order mark, CRLF, and a leading apostrophe on any text that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or carriage return; amounts are plain decimals in rupees so they stay numbers). XLSX is written by a small module over `fflate` rather than a spreadsheet library: text is always an inline string, which a spreadsheet never evaluates as a formula; amounts, quantities, rates and dates are real numbers with number formats; the first row is frozen. Both export every row of the filtered report in batches of 5,000, never only the rows on screen.
- **Print** is the report as a plain table on its own sheet (business, title, period, rows, totals), placed beside the app and the app hidden while it prints, as the receipt does. The prompt asked for a report template in the designer; the designer is about documents, so this is deferred (B-043). A register longer than 3,000 rows prints its first 3,000 and says so.
- **Dashboards** are one read, `getDashboard`, for the caller's date. Shop asks four questions (sold today, who owes me, what is running low, what needs attention); Books asks six (GST this month, cash and receivables, overdue, spend, this month against last month, waiting for review). Every number is a button that opens the matching report with the same period and filters. The GST figure is an estimate: this month's tax on sales less tax on purchases, with all purchase tax treated as credit (B-047). Filing dates are the usual 11th and 20th for monthly filers; extensions are not tracked until the Filing Desk (B-049).

## Consequences

- A report is as fast as its query. The widest realistic period on 50,000 documents is under 0.6 s for every report in Node, the export of 29,000 register rows takes about 1.4 s, and the dashboard about 0.8 s (`packages/db/perf/reports.perf.ts`). The browser's SQLite is slower than Node's; the page shows how long each report took beside its row count.
- The documents summary (GSTR-1 table 13) orders numbers by their trailing digits, so `INV/9` comes before `INV/10`.
- Expense summary groups by the line description, because expenses have no category field yet (B-045). Stock summary reads the item's opening stock plus its movements, which prompt 19 may refine (B-044).
- A CA reviewer signs `docs/compliance/reports-review.md` for the sales register, HSN summary and documents summary against three seeded months.
