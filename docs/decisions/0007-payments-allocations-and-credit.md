# 0007 — Payments, allocations, party credit and receipts

Status: accepted. Context: prompt 16, PRD FR-050 to FR-053, FR-033, UX-05.

## Decision

- **A document's balance is derived, never stored.** `balance = total − paid − credit applied − written off`, summed from `payment_allocations` every time (`PaymentFlowRepo.positionsIn`). Status (unpaid, partial, paid, overpaid) and overdue days come from the domain (`payStatus`, `overdueDays`). Only allocations of _cleared_ payments count, so a bounced or cancelled payment settles nothing. One function feeds the documents list, Party 360, the payment form and the reconciliation test, so they cannot disagree.
- **Three kinds of allocation.** `payment`: money from a payment against a document. `credit_applied`: credit used later, linked to its source (`source_document_id` = the receipt voucher for an advance, or the credit note); a credit note has no payment behind it, so `payment_id` is now optional. `write_off`: a round-off difference closed on the document (not money, not drawn from the payment).
- **What a payment can settle.** Receipts settle tax invoices, bills of supply and debit notes; payments made settle purchase bills and expenses. Credit notes and purchase returns are credit, not things to be paid. The domain checks allocations (`validateAllocations`): never above a document's balance or the amount received, no duplicates, a write-off only up to 99 paise and only when it closes the document.
- **Party credit is whatever is left.** The remainder of a payment after allocations stays on the payment as an advance, and an unused credit note is credit too. `creditSources` lists both, and applying credit (`applyCredit`, offered after issuing a document for a party that has some, oldest first) never takes more than a source has or the document owes. The wording ("credit" or "advance") is a setting.
- **Credit that has been used blocks deleting its source (FR-053).** `remove` refuses with a `CreditAppliedError` listing the documents, unless `force`, which removes those applications so those documents are owed again. Editing a payment cannot take its unallocated amount below what has already been applied from it. Cancelling a credit note unapplies what was applied from it (the cancellation already reports unapplied payments).
- **The ledger is posted by the payment, and reversed on the net.** A receipt debits cash or bank and credits receivables, a payment made does the reverse, an _adjustment_ (a set-off or discount, no money) posts to a new Adjustments account, and a write-off posts to round-off. Editing or deleting reverses the net still standing for the payment (per account and party) and, for an edit, posts again, the same approach as cancelling a document (ADR 0006).
- **TCS on receipt.** `tcsOnReceipt` charges 206C(1H) on the part of the consideration above Rs 50 lakh received in the financial year, including the part of this receipt that crosses it, and is zero for receipts on or after 1 October 2024 or when the buyer deducts TDS. The TCS is part of the amount received but credited to TCS payable, not to the customer (`payments.tcs_paise`; the party balance uses amount less TCS). The form computes it in two passes because the TCS is itself part of the amount (consideration = amount − TCS). This is separate from the invoice-level TCS field the editor already has; use one or the other for a given sale.
- **Receipt vouchers are documents.** Each receipt makes a `receipt_voucher` document with its own series (`RV/{FY}/`), issued in the same transaction, linked from `payments.voucher_id`, with the allocations written into its metadata so it can print on its own. Editing a payment bumps the voucher's revision and keeps the number; deleting cancels it and keeps the number. Payments made have no voucher yet (backlog).
- **Receipts and payments obey the period lock.** A payment dated inside a locked period needs an owner override like any other change (ADR 0006).
- **Reconciliation is a test, not a hope.** The demo seed now posts every issued document and every payment to the ledger, and a test checks, for all 50 seeded parties on both databases, that the party balance engine, the sum of document balances less credit, and the ledger receivables less payables are equal. A second test does the same through a sequence of invoice, partial payment, credit note, overpayment and credit application.
- **Undo is delete.** The toast after recording a payment offers Undo for 30 seconds; it calls `remove`, which is audited like any deletion.
- **Business accounts** (`business_accounts`) gain holder name, account type and a "show on documents" flag; bank accounts validate IFSC and account number, UPI IDs are validated, one account is the default. Receipts print the ones marked to show.
- **Day book** is read from the ledger's cash account (so it agrees with the books): cash in, cash out and the closing balance for a day.

## Consequences

- A migration (the third incremental one) adds the payment and account columns and makes `payment_allocations.payment_id` optional; SQLite rebuilds that table, so it is a few seconds on a very large database.
- Payments have only one status in use (`cleared`). Cheques are recorded as cleared with their number and date; a "pending, bounced" cheque workflow is backlog B-026. The balance logic already ignores payments that are not cleared.
- The allocation form lists only documents with something owed; opening it to edit a payment adds that payment's own allocations back, so a fully paid document can still be re-allocated.
- Receipts print and share as text (WhatsApp); a PDF with the template designer comes with prompt 17.
