# 0033 — Batches, serials, godowns and valuation

Status: accepted. Context: prompt 51, PRD FR-111 and FR-112, on top of ADR 0010 (stock is the sum of movements) and ADR 0032 (the ledger in the open).

## Decision

- **Stock stays the sum of movements; the movements now say which godown, which batch and which serials.** Nothing new stores a quantity. A movement names its godown (older rows without one belong to the default), its batch, and the serial numbers it carried. Stock per godown, per batch and per serial is read from them, like stock itself. A `serials` row per unit says whether it is in stock and where; the movements keep it current, so finding one among a hundred thousand is an index lookup (tested under 100 ms on both dialects).
- **An item says how its units are told apart.** `tracking_mode` is `none`, `batch` or `serial`; a batch item may require an expiry date on every batch; an item may have its own default godown. The mode cannot change while the item holds stock. A purchase of a batch item names the batch (made on the spot in the editor); a sale that names none takes first expiry, first out across the batches in its godown, splitting the line into one movement per batch and passing over expired batches. A serial item moves one unit per serial: a sale needs each serial in stock in that godown, a purchase refuses one that already is, and the editor checks the list against the stock as it is typed or scanned.
- **Transfers never change what the business holds.** A transfer takes stock out of one godown with a `transfer` movement and brings it into another with a second one; dispatched goods are in transit until received (or the dispatch is cancelled and they go back). Business-wide stock and the valuation leave `transfer` movements out, so the total is the same throughout; per-godown stock counts them. A godown with stock cannot be archived; the default cannot be.
- **Valuation is a method the business chooses, and from then on a posting.** Until it chooses, stock is valued at the purchase price and nothing is posted (as before). With FIFO or weighted average chosen, every movement is costed by `runValuation` in `@gst/domain` over the item's history in date order (FIFO keeps layers, weighted average one pool; a return comes back at the current unit cost, a cancellation at exactly what it took, a shortfall at the last known cost) and posted: goods in debit Inventory (1250) and credit Changes in Inventory (5050); goods out, the cost of goods sold, the reverse. Documents post with their own entries (reversed with the document); adjustments and opening stock post a journal voucher against Adjustments or Opening Balance Equity. Purchases still post to Purchases (5000) in full, so the purchase register ties as before, and the profit and loss shows Purchases plus Changes in Inventory, which together are the cost of goods sold (the Schedule III presentation). Choosing a method posts one true-up voucher that brings the Inventory ledger to the stock value; changing it later is a migration: every movement is re-costed under the new method and the difference posted. `ValuationRepo.verify` compares the stock value with the Inventory ledger at any time, and `trueUp` posts the difference (a back-dated purchase can open one).
- **Reports and alerts read the same movements.** Stock value by item, batch or godown as at a date (split at the item's unit cost, the last part taking the rounding), expiry (batches with stock expiring by a date), dead stock (stock that has not sold in the period), stock ageing (what is on hand by the layer it came in on) and a movement register, each per godown where it makes sense; the stock summary gains a godown filter and is valued by the method when one is chosen (B-044). Batches with stock inside ninety days of expiry, or past it, raise an `expiry_due` inbox item once a month per batch.

## Consequences

- An older business keeps working unchanged: its items are `none`, its movements belong to the default godown, and its stock is valued at purchase price until it chooses a method.
- Valuing stock runs the engine over an item's whole history on every read and on every issue; at SME scale that is milliseconds, at tens of thousands of movements per item it will want a cached position (B-228).
- Under FIFO a cancelled purchase takes its units off the oldest layers, not the layer it made; the cost is fixed to what the purchase paid, so the value is right and only the layer ageing is approximate.
- The demo business is unchanged; `?demo=1&pharma=1` adds a pharma-style set (batches with expiry, a
  serial-tracked device, two godowns, FIFO) for the e2e flows, and `seedPharma` builds the same through
  the repositories for the tests. The seed ends with one more sale so the document count (506) says
  when it is all in; a navigation before that cuts it short.
