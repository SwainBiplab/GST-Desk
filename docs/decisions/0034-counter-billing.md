# 0034 — Counter billing (POS)

Status: accepted. Context: prompt 52, PRD FR-113 and FR-114, on top of the document engine (ADR 0005), numbering series (ADR 0012), payments (ADR 0017), inventory (ADR 0010 and 0033) and the ledger (ADR 0032).

## Decision

- **A counter bill is an ordinary invoice, made fast.** It goes through the same `DocumentFlowRepo` as every other bill: the document engine prices it, the number comes from the counter's series, stock moves, the ledger posts, and the same validation applies. There is no second sales path to keep in line with GST. The business's scheme decides the type: a tax invoice, or a bill of supply under composition. B2C sales to a customer with no GSTIN, with the state they are in, reach GSTR-1 as B2CS (or B2CL above the limit) through the rules that already do that.
- **Walk-in is a customer.** A sale with no customer named goes to one "Walk-in customer" party per business (unregistered, in the business's state), made on first use. That keeps payments, credit notes and the party position working with no special case; a customer can still be chosen for a GSTIN, an interstate sale or a credit note.
- **A counter has its own series, and a shift is its unit of cash.** Making a counter makes a number series for it (`C1/0001`, `C2/0001`), so two tills never take each other's numbers, and `PosRepo.reserveNumbers` hands a counter a block of its series' numbers for a device that is cut off (the existing reservation machinery of prompt 41). A shift is one cashier's turn: opened with a float, closed with the cash counted. One shift per counter may be open; sales are refused with none.
- **Payment is split by the rules in `@gst/domain/pos`.** Cash, UPI, card and other can be mixed on one bill; card and UPI settle exactly what they are for (together they cannot exceed the bill), cash settles the rest and anything above is change, so what the books record is exactly the bill, never the cash handed over. Each mode becomes one payment against the invoice. A counter sale makes no receipt voucher of its own (`record` takes `skipVoucher`): the invoice is the document, and a voucher per mode per bill would be noise.
- **A sale is one transaction.** Issue the bill, record the payments and the counter's own record (`pos_bills`: which shift, how it was paid, the change) succeed or fail together; the tenders are checked before anything is written, so a refused payment leaves nothing behind.
- **Returns are credit notes with a refund.** Scanning the invoice's QR (`GSTIN|number|date|total`, which the printed invoice already carries, and which the thermal receipt now prints on request) or typing the number finds the bill; the lines and quantities coming back are chosen, bounded by what has not already come back, and issued as a credit note against it. The refund goes back as cash (out of the drawer) or to the mode paid with, and the shift's expected cash falls by the cash given. What came back is kept on the return by the bill's own line numbers (a credit note numbers its lines afresh), so one bill can be returned in parts.
- **The drawer is arithmetic.** Expected cash is the float, plus cash settled on bills, less cash refunded, plus cash put in and less cash taken out by hand. Closing records the count, the expected figure and the variance (counted less expected) with the shift; the Z report (sales and returns, takings by mode, tax, the drawer) reads the same rows and ties to the books: net takings by mode equal net sales.
- **The screen is built for the scanner.** A USB scanner types fast and ends with Enter (or Tab, or a pause); `WedgeParser` tells a scan from typing by the gaps between keys and by a programmed prefix, so one input box serves both: a scan is looked up by barcode, then SKU, then serial number in an in-memory index (a map lookup, tested well under the 100 ms budget with 50,000 items), typing searches by name, an empty Enter pays, `3*code` sells three, and the invoice QR starts a return. Camera scanning uses the existing barcode module; a serial scale is read through Web Serial; a second window shows the customer the lines and the total over a BroadcastChannel and holds no data of its own.
- **Discounts follow a limit.** Anyone who can approve sales may give any discount; the others up to a limit set for the business (5% to start with). The limit is enforced on the screen; the engine prices whatever discount arrives.

## Consequences

- No new tax or numbering rules were written. Anything wrong with a counter bill is wrong with every bill, and is fixed once.
- A cashier whose permissions allow selling can open a shift and close it; separate "close the day" rights are not modelled (B-238).
- Receipts print to a paired Bluetooth printer as ESC/POS (with the drawer kick), or through the browser's print dialog as the thermal layout. USB raw printing from the desktop app, and so a drawer kick without Bluetooth, is not built (B-239).
- Returns made from the editor rather than the counter are not counted against a bill's returnable quantity (B-240).
- The pilot week with a real counter (`docs/qa/pos-pilot.md`) has not happened; scanner, printer, drawer and scale were tested with simulated input and parsers only.
